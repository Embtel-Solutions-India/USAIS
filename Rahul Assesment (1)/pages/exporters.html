Why Exporters?
Exporters Are the Strongest US Expansion Candidates

Most people who want to move to America must build a case from scratch. As an exporter, your case already exists — in your shipping records, your buyer relationships and your balance sheet.

Tariffs Changed the Mathematics of Exporting

When duties eat your margin, an American presence stops being a dream and becomes a business decision. US-based warehousing, assembly, finishing or manufacturing can reduce landed cost, shorten delivery times and reassure buyers — and the same US company creates the corporate relationship an L-1A visa is built on. Duty rates change frequently; our Feasibility Assessment analyzes exposure for your specific products before you commit.

The Corporate Relationship Is Simple

An L-1A transfer requires a qualifying link between your foreign company and a US entity. For an exporter, that means incorporating a US subsidiary — something many exporters already want for distribution, warehousing or tariff planning.

Your US Demand Is Already Proven

Years of shipping bills, purchase orders and customs records are documentary evidence of genuine American business. A service startup has projections; you have history.

Financial Capacity Is On Record

Export turnover, bank realization certificates and audited accounts demonstrate that your parent company can fund and sustain US operations — a key evidentiary element in new-office petitions.

Tariffs Create a Real Business Purpose

USCIS looks for a genuine reason the US entity exists. Reducing duty exposure through US assembly, finishing or distribution is a business rationale that speaks for itself.

Buyers Prefer a Domestic Counterparty

US retailers and OEMs increasingly favor suppliers with a US entity: faster replenishment, US invoicing, easier returns and compliance. Your US branch is a sales asset, not just a visa vehicle.

A Built-In Green Card Runway

Operate your US company for a year on L-1A and the EB-1C multinational manager green card pathway opens for you and your family.

Questions Exporters Ask
Frequently Asked Questions
I already export successfully. Why do I need a US company?
Five reasons: hold inventory closer to buyers, invoice in USD from a domestic entity, reduce landed-cost and tariff exposure through local assembly or finishing, win buyers who prefer an American counterparty, and create the parent–subsidiary relationship an L-1A intracompany transfer requires. Most exporters find the business case stands on its own even before immigration enters the picture.
How exactly does my export history strengthen an L-1A case?
Your shipping bills, purchase orders and US buyer contracts prove genuine, pre-existing American demand, while your export turnover and audited accounts demonstrate the parent company's ability to fund the new US office. These are precisely the evidentiary areas where new-office petitions are scrutinized — and where exporters hold an advantage over first-time entrants.
Do I have to manufacture in the USA, or can I start with distribution?
Many exporters begin with warehousing and distribution, then add assembly, finishing or full manufacturing as volumes justify it. The right sequence depends on your products, duty exposure and buyer requirements — which is exactly what the Feasibility Assessment maps out for you.
What if my company isn't ready yet?
Then we'll tell you — in writing, with a gap list. Common gaps include a thin management layer below the owner, insufficient documented US revenue, or an unrealistic staffing plan for the US entity. The assessment gives you a build-first roadmap so that when you do proceed, you proceed strong.
Is US Area Immigration Services a law firm?
No. We are a U.S. immigration advisory, not a law firm. We provide the market-entry strategy, US branch establishment, documentation preparation and settlement services around your move — and all visa and green card petitions are prepared and filed by licensed US immigration attorneys we coordinate with.
Can my family come with me?
Yes. Spouses and unmarried children under 21 can accompany an L-1A holder on L-2 status, and spouses are generally eligible to work in the US. Our Full Settlement Package covers the family side — housing, schooling and settling in — alongside the business.
